Random Forest – Concepts

Active Session | v1.0

Definition

Random Forest is a supervised learning algorithm based on the combination of multiple decision trees to improve accuracy and reduce overfitting.

How it Works

It generates many decision trees trained on random subsets of data and features. The final prediction is obtained by voting (classification) or averaging (regression).

Types of Problems

  • Binary and multiclass classification.
  • Regression with continuous variables.
  • Problems with tabular and structured data.

Main Characteristics

  • Use of bootstrap (random samples with replacement).
  • Random selection of variables at each split.
  • Robustness against noise and outliers.

Advantages

  • High accuracy compared to a single tree.
  • Reduces the risk of overfitting.
  • Works well with large datasets.

Limitations

  • Less interpretable than a single tree.
  • Higher computational cost.
  • Not always the best option for very sparse data.

Real-life Example

In a bank, Random Forest can be used to predict whether a customer will approved for a loan. Multiple trees are trained with data such as income, payment history, and age, and the final decision is made by voting of all the trees.
