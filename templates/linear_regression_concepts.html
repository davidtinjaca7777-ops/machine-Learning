What is Linear Regression?

Linear regression is a statistical technique that seeks to represent the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables through a straight line. It is used to understand how changes in the explanatory variables affect the outcome and to make predictions based on historical data.

Types of Problems it Solves

This model is applied in situations where it is necessary to estimate continuous values, such as income, housing prices, or future sales. It also allows identifying trends and quantifying the impact of different factors on an outcome.

Dependent Variable

It is the target variable that we want to predict, typically represented as Y in the equation. Example: monthly income of a company or the price of a product in the market.

Independent Variable

These are the input features or attributes (X) that influence the outcome. Example: number of customers, advertising investment, or store size.

Relationship between Variables

Linear regression assumes that there is a proportional relationship between the independent and dependent variables. This means that a change in X generates a predictable change in Y, following the slope of the fitted line.

Regression Line

The general equation is Y = β₀ + β₁X, where β₀ is the intercept and β₁ is the slope. This line represents the best linear approximation to the observed data, minimizing the prediction error.

Illustration of the regression line: Y = β₀ + β₁X

Prediction

Once the model is trained, the regression line can be used to estimate future values of Y based on new values of X. This allows anticipating results and making strategic decisions based on reliable data.

Simple Example

Let's say we want to predict the revenue of a store based on the number of customers. If the fitted line is Revenue = 50 + 20 × Customers, then with 100 customers we estimate revenue of 2,050 monetary units.
